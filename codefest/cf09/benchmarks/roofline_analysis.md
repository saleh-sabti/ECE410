# CF09 CLLM Roofline Analysis

The hardware accelerator lands at 297.7 MOPS on the sky130 roofline, plotted at both the lower bound arithmetic intensity (AI = 1.5 FLOP/byte, no on-chip reuse) and the upper bound (AI = 192 FLOP/byte, full shift-register reuse). Both points hit the compute ceiling at 297.7 MOPS, meaning the design is compute-bound across its full operating range. This matches expectations: the AXI4-S interface delivers 4 bytes per cycle, but the shift registers retain the previous N-1 samples on chip, so the interface is rarely the bottleneck after the first window fills.

The software baseline lands at 135.8 MOPS with AI = 0.747 FLOP/byte. This is well below the HW compute ceiling and sits in the memory-bound region of a general-purpose CPU. The low AI comes from float32 representation (4 bytes per sample vs. INT16 at 2 bytes), the absence of shift-register reuse, and Python overhead driving down effective compute density.

The gap between where the accelerator landed (297.7 MOPS) and where I expected it is small: the projection assumed N+1 = 129 cycles per window at 50 MHz, which gives 387,597 windows/sec and 297.7 MOPS. The actual synthesis confirmed 50 MHz closed (WNS = 0 at nom_tt). The dominant uncertainty is whether 50 MHz holds after a complete routing run. The DRT routing failure in M3 means the placed-and-routed timing has not been verified, so the 50 MHz figure could degrade under real wire parasitics. Converting this to a measurement would require: (1) completing the routing in a PDK that supports the LEF58_ENCLOSURE mcon rule (OpenLane 2 limitation), (2) extracting RC parasitics, and (3) re-running STA post-route. The throughput number would then be measured rather than projected.
